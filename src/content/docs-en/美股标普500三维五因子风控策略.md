## 1. Model Purpose

This strategy identifies systemic risk in the U.S. equity market, assigns a risk level from 1 to 6, and maps that level to a target equity allocation.

The strategy supports:

- Managing U.S. equity exposure.
- Identifying broad-market systemic risk.
- Controlling drawdowns.
- Gradually increasing exposure as risk recedes.

It is not intended for:

- Precisely timing market tops or bottoms.
- Short-term trading.
- Independently determining individual stock trades.
- Replacing fundamental research.
- Direct application to A-shares, Hong Kong equities, or other markets.

## 2. Market and Data

The default market is U.S. equities, with the S&P 500 as the primary reference.

The strategy uses daily data:

- S&P 500 price index closing level.
- VIX closing level.
- ICE BofA US High Yield OAS.
- U.S. 10-year Treasury yield.
- U.S. 2-year Treasury yield.

Data definitions must remain consistent. High-yield spreads must use `BAMLH0A0HYM2` or a fully equivalent ICE BofA US High Yield OAS series. Do not silently substitute HYG, BAA, investment-grade OAS, or yield levels.

## 3. Risk Levels

| Level | Name           | Interpretation                                          |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Easy Mode      | Low risk and a healthy trend.                           |
| 2     | Normal Mode    | Normal risk; maintain regular allocation.               |
| 3     | Hard Mode      | Risk is rising; limit new exposure.                     |
| 4     | Inferno Mode   | Clearly elevated risk; actively reduce exposure.        |
| 5     | Hell Mode      | Extremely high risk; prioritize defense.                |
| 6     | Nightmare Mode | Crisis-level risk; adopt an extremely defensive stance. |

## 4. Factor Definitions and Weights

All five factors are oriented so that **higher values indicate greater risk**.

| Factor    | Dimension       | Weight | Risk Direction                                                                    |
| --------- | --------------- | -----: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `VIX_Z`   | Sentiment       |    22% | Higher VIX implies greater risk.                                                  |
| `HY_Z`    | Credit          |    18% | Wider high-yield spreads imply greater risk.                                      |
| `TS_Z`    | Yield curve     |    15% | A lower 10Y–2Y spread implies greater risk.                                       |
| `DEV_Z`   | Technical trend |    15% | A larger downward S&P 500 deviation from its moving average implies greater risk. |
| `SLOPE_Z` | Technical trend |    30% | A lower moving-average slope implies greater risk.                                |

Term-spread risk:

```text
TS_RISK = -(DGS10 - DGS2)
```

S&P 500 deviation risk:

```text
MA = 125-trading-day moving average of the S&P 500
DEV_RISK = -((SP500 - MA) / MA)
```

Moving-average slope risk:

```text
SLOPE_RISK = -((current 125-day MA - 125-day MA from 20 trading days ago) / 125-day MA from 20 trading days ago)
```

## 5. Data Processing

- Use daily source data.
- Forward-fill missing non-price factors for at most 5 trading days.
- Do not generate an official risk level when 2 or more factors are missing on the same day.
- Apply rolling 1st/99th percentile winsorization to each factor before standardization.
- Calculate Z-scores using a rolling window of 504 trading days, with a minimum of 252 observations.
- Clip Z-scores to `[-3, 3]`.

## 6. Composite Risk Score

The raw composite risk score is:

```text
risk_score_raw =
0.22 × VIX_Z
+ 0.18 × HY_Z
+ 0.15 × TS_Z
+ 0.15 × DEV_Z
+ 0.30 × SLOPE_Z
```

The final score is its 10-day average:

```text
risk_score = 10-day average of risk_score_raw
```

## 7. Risk Level Thresholds

| Risk Level | Score Range           |
| ---------- | --------------------- |
| 1          | score < -0.75         |
| 2          | -0.75 ≤ score < -0.05 |
| 3          | -0.05 ≤ score < 0.60  |
| 4          | 0.60 ≤ score < 1.20   |
| 5          | 1.20 ≤ score < 1.90   |
| 6          | score ≥ 1.90          |

## 8. Override Rules

Overrides handle cases where one or more key risk factors have deteriorated substantially but the composite score does not yet fully reflect the deterioration.

Single-factor override:

```text
If any of VIX_Z, HY_Z, or SLOPE_Z is ≥ 2.4, the risk level is at least 4.
```

Two-factor resonance override:

```text
If any two of the five factors are ≥ 1.9, the risk level is at least 5.
```

Crisis override:

```text
If VIX_Z ≥ 2.5 and SLOPE_Z ≥ 2.0, the risk level is 6.
If HY_Z ≥ 2.5 and DEV_Z ≥ 2.0, the risk level is 6.
```

## 9. Oversold Recovery Rule

This rule helps avoid remaining at an excessively high risk level after the market has materially recovered.

Candidate condition:

```text
Current risk level ≥ 5
```

At least one of the following oversold conditions must also hold:

```text
DEV_Z ≥ 2.0
or the cumulative S&P 500 return over the past 20 trading days is ≤ -12%
```

All of the following must also hold:

```text
VIX_Z has fallen by at least 0.6 from a local peak within the past 10 trading days.
SLOPE_Z has improved consecutively over the past 5 trading days.
No override has been triggered.
```

Adjustment:

```text
If the qualifying conditions hold for 2 consecutive trading days, lower the risk level by at most 1.
The adjusted risk level cannot be lower than 4.
```

## 10. Weekly Confirmation

Daily risk levels first pass through the override and oversold recovery rules, then enter weekly confirmation.

- Confirmation occurs on Fridays.
- When the risk level rises, adopt the higher level immediately after Friday confirmation.
- By default, a decrease requires 3 consecutive weekly confirmation points in a lower risk level before officially reducing the level.
- If the current official risk level is 5 or 6, a decrease requires only 2 consecutive weekly confirmation points.

This reduces trading frequency and helps avoid increasing exposure prematurely during short-lived rebounds.

## 11. Equity Allocation

| Risk Level | Target Equity Allocation |
| ---------- | -----------------------: |
| 1          |                     100% |
| 2          |                      97% |
| 3          |                      90% |
| 4          |                      67% |
| 5          |                      47% |
| 6          |                      27% |

The mapping reflects a preference for a high Sharpe ratio, fewer trades, and sustained equity exposure: maintain relatively high exposure at low to moderate risk, and reduce exposure without fully exiting at high risk.

## 12. Signal Priority

When multiple signals occur together, resolve them in this order:

```text
Crisis override
> Two-factor resonance override
> Single-factor override
> Composite risk classification
> Oversold recovery adjustment
> Weekly confirmation
```

Overrides take precedence over the composite score. The oversold recovery adjustment applies only when no override has been triggered.
